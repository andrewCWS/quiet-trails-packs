# Story 48 — Leave No Trace

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Billy · **Category:** Trip logistics · **Arc:** How-it-works
**Stretch words:** trace, scrap, vanish
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1050
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** The bins are the thing that catches people out.

There aren't any. Not hidden round the back, not at the entrance, not a big one by the road. Ganguddy has no bins at all, on purpose, and everything you brought in a packet goes home in the car with you, in a bag, on somebody's feet.

"That's *weird*," said Rori, when this was explained on the last morning.

It's not, though. Think about what a bin actually is out here. Somebody would have to drive a truck four hours in and four hours out to empty it. And in between, it would be a large metal box full of food, sitting in the bush, with a lid that people leave open — which is a machine for teaching every animal in the valley that humans mean dinner.

And an animal that learns humans mean dinner stops being able to feed itself properly. It hangs around car parks. It gets sick on bread. It gets braver, and then it gets hit by a car.

So: no bins. Which means the campsite has to **vanish**.

Billy took this extremely seriously, in the way Billy takes things, and made it a job with stages.

**Stage one was the big stuff**, which was easy and mostly involved adults and swearing about tent poles.

**Stage two was the small stuff**, which is where it got interesting. Because once the tents were down, the ground underneath had things on it that nobody remembered putting there. A bottle top. A twist tie. Half a peg. A piece of orange plastic about the size of a fingernail that nobody could identify and which had definitely come off something of theirs.

"Where's this even *from*," said Rori.

Nobody knew. That's the point. Almost nothing anybody leaves behind gets left on purpose. It falls out of a pocket, or blows off a table, and then it's just there, and it stays there for a very long time.

**Stage three was the fire.** Cold ash, spread thin, and the unburnt ends taken home — because a fire that gets buried while it's still warm can smoulder underground for days.

And **stage four was the food**, which is the one people argue about. An apple core is natural, so surely you can throw it in the bush?

No. It takes months to break down. In the meantime it's a bright, sugary, easy meal sitting in a place where nothing like it grows, and the animal that finds it will come back and look for another one. Every **scrap** goes in the bag. Apple cores, crusts, the bit of sausage nobody wanted, and — Billy was firm about this — the tea leaves.

Skye conducted a full search of the site on her hands and knees, narrating, and found eleven things, four of which were hers.

Adi found a bottle top and put it in the bag and then took it out again and looked at it and put it back in, and this happened four times, and everybody let it.

And then Billy did the thing that made it a story instead of a chore. He stood at the edge of the flat bit where the tents had been, and he said: "Right. Look at it. Could you tell?"

And everybody looked.

There was flattened grass, which would stand up again in a couple of days. There was a ring of stones with cold grey ash in it, which had been there before they came and would be there after. And there was nothing else at all.

You could not tell that five people had lived there for three days.

"That's the whole thing," said Billy. "That's the actual whole thing."

From the leaf litter at the edge of the clearing, a voice.

**ECHO:** It's better than it was.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing under the trees, watching them pack the car. That's Echo. She'd been about all morning, which is unusual for her, and nobody had gone especially quiet.

**ECHO:** You found the orange bit. Most people don't find the orange bit.

**NARRATOR:** "There's still stuff," said Billy. "There's stuff in the grass we didn't get."

**ECHO:** There is. There always is. You did the ninety.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the leaves, turned something over, ate it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and this one works anywhere, not only here.

Wherever you stop next — a park, a beach, the side of a road, the place you have lunch — find one piece of rubbish that isn't yours. One. And take it with you.

Not all of it. You'll never get all of it and trying will make you miserable.

One piece, every place you stop. That's it. That's the mission.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone, without a sound.

They found four on the drive home, at two different stops.

Adi found the first one and would not let anybody else carry it.
