# Story 24 — Adi Says Goodbye

**Matrix:** story 49 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Refrain · **Key kid:** Adi · **Category:** Going home
**Stretch words:** none — this one is a saying-goodbye story
**Feeling:** none named
**Word count:** ~700 (~4:00). Refrain stories are short on purpose.
**Refrain:** "Bye."

---

**NARRATOR:** The car was packed and everybody was in it except Adi.

Adi was standing in the middle of the empty campsite with her red bag over one shoulder, not moving, looking at things.

"Adi. Car."

Adi did not come to the car. Adi walked over to the ring of stones where the fire had been, and stood in front of it, and looked at it for a moment.

"Bye," said Adi.

Then she walked to the tree with the smooth pale bark and the scribbles on it, and put her hand flat on it, the way you'd say goodbye to a horse.

"Bye."

Then she went to the water.

This took a while, because the water is a long way down over rocks and Adi does everything herself. When she got there she stood at the edge with her shoes on and looked at the green going out and out between the orange walls.

"Bye," said Adi.

Somebody said something about the car again, from a long way off, and was ignored by everybody including the person who said it.

Because by then Rori had got out and gone down after her, and had not said anything about hurrying up, and was standing next to her at the edge of the water.

So Adi carried on.

She said bye to the rock she'd been sitting on all week, which was just a rock, and was hers.

She said bye to a stick that had been very important on Tuesday and had since been abandoned.

She said bye to a hole in the ground that a lizard had gone into once, three days ago, and had not come out of since, and which Adi had checked on every single day.

"Bye," said Adi, to the hole.

She said bye to the long drop toilet, which nobody expected, and which made Billy laugh so hard he had to sit down on the ground.

She said bye to somebody else's caravan.

She said bye to a bird that wasn't there.

And then she came back up the hill, holding Rori's hand, and stopped at the top and turned around and looked at the whole thing at once — the rocks, the water, the trees, the flat patch of squashed grass where a tent had been and would not be by tomorrow.

She thought about it.

"Bye," said Adi.

Then she got in the car and did her own seatbelt, badly, and had it done properly by somebody else, and went to sleep before the gravel finished.

Here's the thing about what Adi did, and it isn't a small thing.

Everybody else left. Adi *finished*.

There's a difference, and you feel it about an hour down the road. If you just get in the car, the place stays behind you like a room you walked out of. If you go round and say goodbye to it — out loud, to the actual things, in front of your family, even the toilet — then you've closed it properly, and what you take home is a whole thing instead of an interrupted one.

Two-year-olds know this. Everybody else forgets it somewhere around the age of six and then spends the rest of their life feeling vaguely unfinished about places.

So here's a mission, and you do this one at the end.

Before you get in the car — before, not after — go and say goodbye to three things. Out loud.

Not people. Things. The fire. A tree. The water. A rock you sat on. A hole a lizard went into.

Say it properly, and say it out loud, and let everybody hear you.

"Bye."
