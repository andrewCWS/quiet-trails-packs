# Story 52 — Poles, Pegs and Tangles

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Rori · **Category:** Camp craft · **Arc:** Overcoming a hard thing
**Stretch words:** thread, angle, tension
**Feeling:** frustrated, named once
**Word count:** ~1050
**Note:** matrix slot C2. Numbered 52 to keep published episode numbers stable.
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** The pole would not go in.

It was one of those poles made of hollow sections with a stretchy cord running down the middle, so that when you shake it out it more or less assembles itself, which is very satisfying and takes about four seconds.

Getting it *through the tent* took considerably longer.

Rori had volunteered, loudly, before anybody else could. Rori volunteers for things at a speed that leaves no room for discussion. So Rori had the pole, and Rori was feeding it into the long fabric sleeve that runs over the top of the tent, and it had gone about a third of the way and stopped.

She pushed harder. It bent.

She pulled it back a bit and pushed again. It bent in a different place.

She pushed extremely hard, and one of the sections came apart inside the sleeve, which meant there was now a pole in two pieces somewhere in the middle of a tent, connected by a piece of elastic, in the dark of a fabric tunnel where nobody could see it.

Rori said a word.

Then she sat down on the grass next to a tent that was more of a puddle than a tent, and her ears went hot, and everything in her arms wanted to throw the pole into the river.

There's a feeling that lands when a job you said you'd do turns out to be harder than it looked, in front of people. It sits high up in your chest and it makes your eyes sting and it is furious about something that isn't really the pole. That feeling is called being **frustrated**.

She did not throw the pole in the river. She held onto it very tightly and said nothing, which is a lot harder.

Billy came over. Billy did not say *let me do it*, which was the correct decision and possibly saved his life.

"It's the corner," he said.

"What?"

"You're pushing it round the corner. It won't go round a corner if you push. It goes round a corner if you *pull.*"

And that is the entire secret of tent poles, and almost nobody is told it.

When you push a long thin thing, all the force goes into the middle of it, and the middle is exactly where it wants to bend. Push harder and you don't get more pole through the sleeve — you get more bend. That's why it comes apart. You aren't fighting the tent. You're fighting the pole.

So you go round the other side, take hold of the end that's already through, and **thread** it — pull it gently, keeping the **angle** shallow, letting the sleeve do the turning instead of the pole.

They found the loose section by squeezing along the sleeve from the outside, like finding a knot in a shoelace by feel. Rori pushed the two ends together through the fabric, which took three goes and a lot of tongue sticking out, and then Billy pulled from his end and Rori guided from hers, and the pole went through the whole way in about eleven seconds.

Then came the pegs, which are their own thing.

A tent isn't held up by poles. It's held up by **tension** — the poles push out, the pegs hold in, and the tent is the argument between them. Which is why a peg goes in at a slant, leaning *away* from the tent, so that when the guy rope pulls, it pulls the peg deeper into the ground instead of straight out of it.

Rori did four. The first one bent, because she hit it on the head at the wrong angle. The second went in and then came straight back out when the rope went tight, because she'd put it in leaning the wrong way. The third was perfect. The fourth was also perfect, and slightly quicker.

She checked the third one again about an hour later when nobody was looking.

That night the wind came up and moved the canvas around and nothing came out of the ground.

**ECHO:** You nearly threw it.

**NARRATOR:** There was a brown bird in the leaf litter at the edge of the light, standing very still. That's Echo. She turns up for people who have gone quiet, and there had been a solid ninety seconds where Rori was much too angry to speak, which counts.

**ECHO:** Everybody nearly throws it. The ones who get good at poles are the ones who sat down first.

**NARRATOR:** "It bent," said Rori.

**ECHO:** It'll bend again. Pull, don't push. Same as most things.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the leaves, found something, ate it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's a doing one.

Put in one peg yourself. All of it. Nobody holding it for you.

Lean it away from the tent. Hit the top square. And when it's in, take hold of the rope and pull — properly pull — and feel whether it moves.

If it moves, do it again. Everybody does it again. That's not the mission going wrong, that's the mission.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone, without a sound.
