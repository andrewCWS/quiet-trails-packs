# Story 22 — The Stars Nobody Owns

**Matrix:** story 47 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Rori · **Category:** Weather & sky
**Stretch words:** constellation, pointers, horizon
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1060
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission, and refuses something.

---

**NARRATOR:** At home you can see about nine stars.

Nobody tells you that. You look up from the driveway and there are a few, and one of them is probably a plane, and that's the sky and everybody gets on with it. The reason is streetlights — every light on every corner throws a bit of itself upwards, and the whole town glows faintly, and against that glow only the brightest stars can be seen at all.

Out here there are no streetlights. There are no lights of any kind, except a fire that had gone down to orange.

Rori lay on her back on a rock that was still warm from the day, and looked up, and stopped being able to speak.

There were thousands.

Not hundreds. Thousands, in layers, big ones and small ones and a dust of ones so faint they only existed if you looked slightly to the side of them. And running right across the middle, from one **horizon** to the other, a wide pale band like somebody had smudged the sky with a thumb.

"What's the smudge?"

That's the Milky Way. It's the galaxy we live in, seen edgeways, from the inside. Every one of those points of light is a sun, and the smudge is what happens when there are so many of them so far away that your eyes give up on separating them and just show you the glow.

Rori did not say anything for a while.

"How many?"

Too many to count, and that's not a dodge — people have tried, and the answer is in the hundreds of billions, which is a number that doesn't mean anything to anybody.

Billy wanted a system immediately, because Billy always wants a system.

So they found the Southern Cross, which is the easy one, and the trick for finding it is not to look for a cross. Look for four bright stars in a kite shape, with a fifth fainter one off to the side. And then — this is the bit that makes it certain — look for the two very bright stars sitting near it, one after the other, called the **Pointers**. Draw a line through the Pointers and it takes you to the Cross. They travel together across the sky all night, turning slowly, like something on a wheel.

Once you've got it, you can't lose it. That's the thing about a **constellation**. It's not really a thing in the sky. It's a shape people agreed on, thousands of years ago, so they could talk to each other about which bit they meant — and once someone shows you one, you'll find it for the rest of your life.

Skye found a satellite, which is a star that moves in a straight line and doesn't twinkle, and narrated its entire journey across the sky.

Adi pointed at the brightest thing available and said "moon." It was not the moon. It was Venus, low over the ridge. Adi was, once again, closer than anybody had any right to expect.

And then, from the dark under the trees, a voice.

**ECHO:** There's an emu.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing in the leaf litter where it was too dark to see her properly. That's Echo. She comes for people who've gone still, and four children flat on their backs on a warm rock are about as still as children get.

"Where?" said Rori.

**ECHO:** Not in the stars. In the dark bits between them. Look at the Cross, then follow the dark lane along the Milky Way. There's a head, and a long neck, and a body.

**NARRATOR:** And there is. Once somebody points it out, an enormous emu made of darkness lies across the middle of the sky, and it is impossible to unsee.

"Tell us about it," said Rori.

**ECHO:** No.

**NARRATOR:** Just like that. No.

**ECHO:** There are stories about that emu. Good ones. They belong to the people of this Country, and they're not mine to tell — I only copy sounds, and those stories aren't sounds, they're somebody's. It's not a sad thing. It's a good thing. It means they're still theirs.

You can look at it. Looking is fine. Looking is what it's for.

**NARRATOR:** Rori looked at it for a long time.

"Can I find out?" she said. "The stories. Somewhere else."

**ECHO:** Yes. From the right people, who'll be glad you asked.

**NARRATOR:** Then she scratched at the leaves, found something, and ate it, because even at moments like this a bird is a bird.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's the easiest one and the hardest one.

Find one **constellation** — any one, the Cross will do, the Pointers count. Learn it well enough to find it again on your own.

Then go home and find it from your own back yard. It'll be harder there, and smaller, and it will be the same one.

That's the whole mission: to have one shape in the sky that's yours to find.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone.

Rori found the Cross from the driveway at home, four nights later, on the third try, with nine stars to work with.
