# Story 5 — Adi Packs a Bag

**Matrix:** story 5 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Refrain · **Key kid:** Adi · **Category:** Trip prep
**Stretch words:** none — this one is a shouting story
**Feeling:** none named. It's a happy one.
**Word count:** ~640 (~3:30). Refrain stories are short on purpose.
**Refrain:** IN THE BAG!

---

**NARRATOR:** Adi is two.

Adi has her own bag. It is small and red and it has a zip that goes round in a circle, and it is hers, and nobody helps.

Somebody had already packed Adi's bag once that morning. Adi had watched them do it. Then, when they left the room, Adi unpacked the whole thing onto the floor and started again properly.

Here's what went in.

A spoon. Not a special spoon. A spoon.

"IN THE BAG!" said Adi.

A sock. One sock. The other sock stayed where it was, because the other sock was not invited.

"IN THE BAG!"

A rock. Grey, with a white line round the middle, which Adi had taken off Rori's pile without asking, and which Rori would notice in about four hours, in the car, at speed.

"IN THE BAG!"

A book with no cover on it. The cover had come off a long time ago. It was better without.

"IN THE BAG!"

A torch that doesn't work. Everybody knows it doesn't work. Adi knows it doesn't work. Adi turns it on and off anyway, and says "light," and it isn't, and that's fine.

"IN THE BAG!"

A biscuit.

Now — a biscuit is a serious item, and Adi held it for a while first. She looked at the biscuit. She looked at the bag. She looked at the biscuit.

"IN THE BAG," said Adi.

And a duck. A rubber duck, yellow, slightly chewed on one wing, who had been to every single place Adi had ever been.

"IN THE BAG!"

Then Adi did the zip. It went all the way round in one go, which is a very good sound, and she sat on the bag with both hands on it like somebody guarding treasure.

"Adi, that's not — " said Billy, coming past, "there aren't any *clothes* in there."

Adi looked at him.

"Adi, you need clothes."

"IN THE BAG," said Adi, which was not an answer, and also was.

So somebody quietly put some clothes in a different bag, and everybody let the red bag be, and the red bag came camping.

Here's what happened to what was in it.

The spoon got used, at breakfast, for actual eating, and was the only spoon anyone could find.

The sock went missing on day one and has never been seen since.

The rock went in the river, on purpose, by Adi, who watched it sink and said "bye."

The book with no cover got read twice by torchlight, using somebody else's torch, because Adi's torch does not work.

And on the second night, when Rori was cold and grumpy and had gone quiet in the way that means don't talk to me — Adi got up, and went and got her bag, and did the zip all the way round, and took out the biscuit.

She had carried it four hours in a car and two whole days in a bag.

She gave it to Rori.

The duck is still going. The duck goes everywhere.

So here's a mission, and it's a shouting one, so everybody has to join in.

Think of the thing you would put in your bag that nobody else would think of. Not clothes. Not the sensible thing. The *strange* thing.

Say it out loud — and everybody else has to shout —

"IN THE BAG!"
