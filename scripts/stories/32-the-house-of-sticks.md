# Story 32 — The House of Sticks

**Matrix:** story 44 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Adi · **Category:** Activities · **Arc:** Quest
**Stretch words:** lean, ridge, shelter
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1060
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the last mission of the camp.

---

**NARRATOR:** It began because Adi went and stood inside a tree.

Not up it. Inside it. There was a big old gum near the edge of the campsite with a branch that had come down years ago and landed leaning against the trunk, and underneath that branch was a triangle of space about the size of a wardrobe, with leaves on the floor.

Adi walked in, sat down, and said, "House."

Then she went and got her red bag and brought it in, which settled the matter.

Within twenty minutes there were four children in the bush arguing about architecture.

Here's how a stick cubby actually works, as opposed to how everyone first tries to build one.

Everyone's first attempt is a wall. You stand sticks up in a line, and they fall over, and you stand them up again, and they fall over again, and eventually somebody says this is stupid.

The reason is that a stick standing on its own has nothing holding it. The trick is to stop building walls and start building a **lean**.

You find one strong thing that's already there and isn't going anywhere — a fallen branch resting on a trunk, a log, a fork in a tree. That's your **ridge**: the long bone down the middle that everything else hangs off.

Then you take your sticks and lean them against it, from the ground up, at an angle, all the way along both sides. Each one is falling over — but it's falling *onto* the ridge, and the ridge is going nowhere. So they all hold each other up.

That's a lean-to. It's the shape people have used for **shelter** for as long as there have been people, and a five-year-old will build one in ten minutes once somebody points at the ridge.

Billy found the ridge, because Billy finds ridges. Rori did most of the carrying, because Rori is strong and enjoys being told a thing is heavy. Skye directed, which she is unbelievably good at and which nobody thanked her for at the time.

And Adi's actual contribution was the doorway.

Everybody had leaned sticks along the whole length of both sides, and the result was a very fine, very complete, entirely sealed triangle with no way in. Four children stood outside their house looking at it.

Adi walked up to one end, took hold of one stick, and pulled it out, and then another, and then stood back.

Door.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"That's — " said Billy.

"Yeah," said Rori.

Then they thatched it, which is a good word and means laying leafy branches over the frame from the bottom upwards, each layer overlapping the one below like tiles, so water runs down the outside instead of finding its way in. They used bark and dry fern and a great deal of enthusiasm.

It rained for about ten minutes late that afternoon, which was extremely lucky, and all four of them crammed inside it and found out that:

- It mostly worked.
- The bit above Skye did not work.
- It is very loud inside a stick house in the rain, and this is the best thing about it.

They were in there for an hour and a half. Adi's bag was in there. The rules of the house were established, revised, and disputed. Somebody was briefly banished.

**ECHO:** You put the door in.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing just outside, looking in through the doorway that Adi made. That's Echo. She does not come inside things.

**ECHO:** They'd have taken the roof off. They always take the roof off.

**NARRATOR:** "House," Adi explained.

**ECHO:** It is.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the leaves by the entrance, found something, ate it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's the biggest one, and you'll need everybody.

Build a house out of sticks.

Don't start with the walls. Find the **ridge** first — something already fallen, already leaning, already strong. Then lean everything onto it.

Thatch it from the bottom up, so the water runs off instead of in.

And when you've finished, before anybody gets excited, check that you've left a door.

Then take it apart before you go home, and put the sticks back on the ground where they were, because somebody lives under those.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone, without a sound.

They took it apart on the last morning. It took four minutes, which seemed wrong.

Adi said bye to it.
