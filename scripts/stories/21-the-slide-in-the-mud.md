# Story 21 — The Slide in the Mud

**Matrix:** story 17 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Rori · **Category:** Animals · **Arc:** Patience
**Stretch words:** burrow, dusk, evidence
**Feeling:** not named. This is one of the ones that doesn't resolve.
**Word count:** ~1050
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** Rori found the slide by falling over near it.

That's how a lot of discoveries happen and nobody puts it in the books. She'd been going along the edge of the water where the bank gets steep, and her foot went out from under her, and she landed on her hands in cold mud and said something.

And then, right in front of her face, about an arm's length away, was a groove.

It came down the bank in a smooth curve and went into the water. It was about as wide as a dinner plate. The mud in it was polished — properly shiny, the way a slippery dip goes shiny — and it had no footprints on it at all.

"Something *slides* here," said Rori.

She got Billy. Billy looked at it for a long time and did the thing where he narrows his eyes.

"It's been used a lot," he said. "That's not one slide. You don't get shiny from one."

And a little further along, half hidden under a tree root, there was a hole. Round, about as wide as a grapefruit, going back into the bank just above the waterline. The dirt around the entrance was smoothed off.

That's a **burrow**. And a slide plus a burrow above the waterline plus no footprints on the mud means one animal.

Platypus.

Nobody said the word for a bit, in case saying it used it up.

Here's why they weren't going to see one at two o'clock in the afternoon.

A platypus is mostly a **dusk** animal. It feeds in the low light at either end of the day and often right through the night, and it spends the bright middle hours asleep in a burrow in the bank — usually a short one for resting, and sometimes a much longer one, several metres in, dug by a female with eggs.

It dives, feeds along the bottom, comes up, floats, chews, and dives again. Each dive is under a minute. And when it surfaces, it doesn't leap or splash. It appears, quietly, and makes a ring on the water about the size of a dinner plate.

Which means the entire skill is this: you have to be looking at the right patch of water at the right thirty seconds, in bad light, without moving.

So they came back at dusk.

All four of them, plus a grown-up, sitting on the rocks above the slide with the light going orange and then grey. Nobody talked. Billy had made everybody agree to it beforehand, with terms.

Here is what happened over the next forty minutes.

A duck. Twice.

A fish rising, which makes a ring almost exactly like the ring they were waiting for, and got everybody four separate times.

A stick floating past that was, for approximately eleven seconds, absolutely definitely a platypus.

A dragonfly. Several bats, high up, going about their business in a jerky sort of way.

The water going from green to silver to a flat sheet of nothing.

And no platypus.

Rori sat perfectly still for the last twenty minutes of it, which is a very long time for Rori and would have been unimaginable a week earlier.

"It's not coming," she said, eventually.

No. Not tonight.

There's a particular flatness that comes when you did everything right and it didn't happen anyway. It isn't the same as failing. There's nothing you'd do differently. It's just how waiting ends sometimes.

**ECHO:** She was there.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing in the leaf litter behind them, and had been for some time. That's Echo.

"Where?" said Rori.

**ECHO:** Down the far end, past the reeds. I heard her go in.

**NARRATOR:** "We were watching the *wrong bit.*"

**ECHO:** You were watching the bit with her slide on it. That was the right bit. She just went out the other door.

**NARRATOR:** Rori thought about that for a while.

**ECHO:** You sat still for forty minutes. Two nights ago you couldn't do four.

And the slide's still there. She used it this morning, she'll use it tomorrow, and she'll be using it long after you've gone home.

That's the good part. You know where she lives now. Not a guess — you've got the **evidence**. You could come back in ten years and knock.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched twice at the leaves, found something, ate it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and I'm not promising you anything.

Go back to the water at **dusk** — the real dusk, when the light's nearly gone and you can still see the far bank.

Sit down. Pick one patch of water, about as wide as a car, and watch only that.

Watch for a ring with no splash. Not a fish jumping. A ring that arrives quietly and then a small dark bump in the middle of it.

You probably won't see one. Go anyway.

**NARRATOR:** They went back the next night, and the night after.

On the third night, Skye saw it — just for a moment, a ring and a bump and then nothing, right at the far end, in the last of the light.

Rori missed it. She was looking at the wrong end, at a duck.

She made Skye describe it four times, and then went back down the next night anyway, on her own, and sat in the dark for half an hour.
