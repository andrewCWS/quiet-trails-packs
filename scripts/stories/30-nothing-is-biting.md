# Story 30 — Nothing Is Biting

**Matrix:** story 42 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Rori · **Category:** Activities · **Arc:** Patience
**Stretch words:** float, bite, still
**Species:** redfin (introduced) — the fish you're most likely to see in the weir
**Feeling:** impatient, named once
**Word count:** ~1070
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** The float sat on the water and did nothing.

It was red on top and white underneath and about the size of a plum, and it had been sitting in exactly the same spot for eleven minutes, turning very slowly, going nowhere.

Rori watched it with her whole face.

"Is it broken?"

"It's a float. It floats."

"But nothing's *happening.*"

Nothing was happening. That is, in fact, what fishing is, and nobody tells children this before they start, because if they did nobody would ever start.

Here's the honest version.

Fishing is mostly waiting. On a good day, something happens maybe once an hour. On a normal day, nothing happens at all, and you go home and somebody asks if you caught anything and you say no, and that was still the good afternoon.

The float is there to tell you the moment something *does* — a **bite** shows up as a dip, or a twitch, or the float suddenly lying flat, or going under altogether. The whole apparatus exists to make a very small event visible from the bank.

Which means the entire skill is watching a red-and-white plum not move, for a long time, without going away.

Rori lasted eleven minutes and then had to stand up and walk around, because there is a hot fizzing feeling that arrives when nothing is happening and it gets into your knees first. That feeling is called being **impatient**, and Rori has more of it than most people, and it is not a fault — it is the same engine that gets her first into cold water and up rocks nobody else will climb.

It's just extremely inconvenient at a lake.

She came back. Sat down. Watched the float. Lasted about four minutes.

Got up. Threw a stick. Was told, mildly, not to throw sticks near the line.

Sat down.

And somewhere around the forty-minute mark, without deciding to, she stopped noticing that she was waiting.

The float was still doing nothing. But now she could see other things: a water skater making dents in the surface without breaking it. A line of tiny bubbles coming up from somewhere near the reeds. The way the reflection of the rock wall broke up and put itself back together every time a bit of wind came through. A redfin — a real one, small, with dark bars down its side — turning under the surface about two metres from her float, entirely uninterested in it.

She stopped talking about the float and started reporting.

"There's a redfin."

"Where?"

"There. There's another one. They're just — there's heaps, they're just not eating it."

That's usually the answer. The fish are nearly always there. They are simply not hungry, or not hungry for that, or not hungry there.

Redfin are the ones you're most likely to see here, and they're not from here — they were brought out from England a long time ago and they liked it. Which is why, if you ever catch one, it doesn't go back in.

Billy got a bite at about an hour, and lost it, and was philosophical in a way that fooled nobody.

Skye caught a stick.

Adi held the rod for forty seconds, declared it hers permanently, was persuaded otherwise, and spent the rest of the afternoon dropping small stones into the water precisely where the line was, with enormous cheerfulness.

And nobody caught a fish. Not one, all afternoon, by anybody.

Rori sat there for two and a half hours.

**ECHO:** You've got better at this.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing in the leaves behind the rock. That's Echo. She comes for people who have gone **still**, and there was now a considerable amount of stillness available.

**ECHO:** Last week you couldn't do four minutes.

**NARRATOR:** "We didn't catch anything," said Rori.

**ECHO:** No.

**NARRATOR:** "At all."

**ECHO:** No. You sat by a lake for an afternoon and watched everything in it. Some people call that fishing and some people call that nothing, and it is exactly the same afternoon either way.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the leaves, found something, ate it, which was more than anybody else had managed.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's the hardest one I've got.

Sit and watch one thing for ten whole minutes. A float if you've got one. A patch of water if you haven't. A puddle. A bit of sky.

Ten minutes, no getting up, no talking about how long it's been.

Then say three things you saw that weren't there when you started.

There will be three. There are always three. They were there the whole time.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone, without a sound.

Rori's three were: the redfin turning, the bubbles by the reeds, and a bird she couldn't name that walked on the very tops of the lily pads without going in.
