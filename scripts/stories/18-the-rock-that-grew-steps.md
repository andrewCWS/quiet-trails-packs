# Story 18 — The Rock That Grew Steps

**Matrix:** story 13 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Billy · **Category:** Rock & geology · **Arc:** Mystery
**Stretch words:** ancient, erode, band
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1070
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** The rock had steps in it, and nobody had built them.

That was Billy's whole problem, and it took him most of an afternoon.

The rock stood up out of the bush behind the campsite, orange going on gold, about as tall as a house, and it wasn't smooth. It went up in shelves — a wide bit, then a step in, then a narrower bit, then a step in again, all the way to a rounded top. Like a wedding cake. Like a stack of plates that somebody had nudged.

There were more of them further along, dozens, all doing the same thing.

"Somebody made those," said Rori, who was already climbing one.

"Nobody made those," said Billy.

"They're *steps.*"

"They go all the way round. Nobody makes steps that go all the way round a rock for no reason."

He was right, and it bothered him for another hour.

He did what Billy does, which is walk up close and look at the actual thing instead of the idea of it. And up close, the rock was not one rock. It was hundreds of thin layers stacked on top of each other, and you could put your finger along the lines.

Some of the layers stuck out. Some were tucked back in. And the ones that stuck out were a slightly different colour — darker, ruddier, harder-looking. When he knocked on one it made a sharp sound. When he knocked on the soft pale stuff in between, his knuckle came away with orange dust on it.

"They're not the same," he said.

That's the answer, and here's the whole of it.

That rock is sandstone, and it is **ancient** — laid down as sand, in water, roughly two hundred million years ago, long before there were people or gum trees or birds of any kind. Layer after layer after layer, settling flat, one on top of the last, until it was buried and pressed into stone.

But the layers aren't identical. Some of them got iron in them, and iron makes a **band** of rock that's much harder than its neighbours.

Then time gets to work. Wind and rain **erode** stone — wear it away, grain by grain, over an unimaginably long stretch. The soft pale layers wear back quickly. The hard iron bands wear back slowly. So the hard ones end up sticking out, sitting there like the lip of a shelf, protecting the soft layer underneath for a while longer.

Do that for a few million years and you get steps. Nobody built them. They *arrived*, because some layers were tougher than others.

"So the steps are the strong bits," said Billy.

The steps are the strong bits.

And the round tops? Water. Rain sits on top and works its way down into cracks, and rounds off the corners the way a hand rounds off a bar of soap. Which is why the rocks look like beehives, or pagodas, or a giant's stacked pancakes, depending on who's describing them.

Billy told everybody. Twice. Rori did not care at all and continued climbing, which is a completely reasonable response to a rock.

Skye cared enormously and immediately began looking for the *thinnest* band she could find, and found one about as thick as a biscuit, and made everyone come and look at it, and was right that it was excellent.

And Adi put her hand flat on the warm orange stone and said, "Big."

Which is also correct.

Then, from somewhere in the leaf litter behind them, a voice.

**ECHO:** You're counting.

**NARRATOR:** There was a brown bird standing in the leaves with a tail like an untidy feather duster. That's Echo. She turns up when people have gone quiet, and Billy had been quiet, on and off, for about an hour.

**ECHO:** Everyone climbs them. Almost nobody counts them.

**NARRATOR:** "How many are there?" said Billy.

**ECHO:** More than you'd think. Fewer than you'd guess if you guessed twice.

**NARRATOR:** Which is not an answer, and is very much how Echo talks.

She scratched at the leaves, turned something over, ate it, and looked up.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's a slow one.

Find a rock with steps in it. Put your hand on the bottom **band** and count upwards. Every layer that sticks out. Say the numbers out loud so somebody else can check.

Then find the thinnest one. That layer took just as long as the fat ones did. It was just a quieter year.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone, without a sound, which for a bird who can copy any sound at all is a very good joke.

Billy counted nineteen.

Rori counted twenty-three, from the top down, upside down, hanging off the side.

Skye counted twenty-one and then started again because she'd lost her place talking.

Nobody agreed, which is normal, and which is why the mission is worth doing more than once.
