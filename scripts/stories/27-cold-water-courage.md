# Story 27 — Cold Water Courage

**Matrix:** story 40 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Rori · **Category:** Activities · **Arc:** Overcoming fear
**Stretch words:** gasp, adjust, shallow
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1060
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** The water was colder than anybody had agreed to.

It had looked warm. That's the trick of it — green water in bright sun, with the rock going orange all round it and the light coming off the surface, looks like a bath. Everybody had walked down there in the middle of the day fully intending to get in.

Then Rori put one foot in up to the ankle and made a sound.

"It's FINE," she announced, from the **shallow** bit, with one foot in, not moving.

It was not fine. It was the kind of cold that goes straight up your leg bones.

Here's why the water at Ganguddy is colder than it looks, and it's worth knowing before you promise anybody anything.

It's deep, and it's shaded for a good part of the day by high rock walls, and it doesn't move much. Sun warms the top layer, but the top layer is thin and the cold sits underneath it. So you can be standing in warm water at your knees and step forward into something completely different at your waist. There's often an actual line where it changes, and you can feel it with your hand.

Billy went in first, which surprised everyone including Billy. He did it by walking, steadily, without stopping and without any announcements, all the way to his chest, and then standing there with a peculiar expression.

"How is it," said Skye.

"It's — " said Billy. There was a pause. "It's *good.*"

He wasn't lying, and he wasn't being brave about it either. Something real had happened, and it happens to everybody, and this is the part nobody explains to children.

When cold water hits your skin, your body panics for about thirty seconds. Your chest tightens. You **gasp** — an actual involuntary gulp of air you didn't decide to take. Your breathing goes fast and shallow and your heart speeds up.

That's not you being scared. It's an automatic reaction, and it happens to grown-ups and to people who swim in cold water on purpose every day of their lives.

And it passes. Within about a minute your breathing settles, your skin stops shrieking, and your body **adjusts**. After that, cold water stops being an emergency and starts being — genuinely — one of the best feelings available to a person.

The whole difficulty is the minute.

The standard method is to get it over with, which is what Billy did, and which works if you can make yourself do it. Rori could not make herself do it. Rori, who goes first into everything, was stuck in fifteen centimetres of water with her arms crossed, going nowhere, and getting crosser about it because going first is meant to be *her* thing.

So Skye, who is not a swimmer and had no intention of getting past her knees, sat down in the shallow bit next to her and said: "Do it in bits."

Ankles. Then stand there and count to ten.

Knees. Count to ten.

The horrible one, which everybody knows about and nobody talks about, which is the bit between your knees and your middle. Count to ten. Rori got to four and said several words.

Then in, all at once, because by then the rest is easier than standing there.

She came up gasping and shouting, in the deep green, about four metres out.

Then, about forty seconds later, she stopped shouting, and her face changed, and she said, "oh."

That's the minute passing. That's all that is.

She was in for an hour and a half. She had to be got out, twice.

**ECHO:** You went second.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing on the rock above the water where the shade starts. That's Echo, and she does not swim, and has strong feelings about it.

**ECHO:** You don't like going second.

**NARRATOR:** "I don't," said Rori.

**ECHO:** You did it anyway. That's the harder one, and nobody claps for it.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the moss on the rock, found nothing, and looked mildly annoyed about it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's a counting-out-loud one, and everybody has to hear you.

At the edge, in the shallow, count to three. Out loud, so it's real.

Then go. Not all the way if you don't want to — knees is a whole mission on its own.

Then stay in for one minute, and count that out loud too, with somebody counting with you.

If you're still cold at the end of the minute, get out. If you're not, you won't want to.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone, without a sound.

Billy claims he counted to three. Nobody heard it.
