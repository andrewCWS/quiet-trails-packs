# Story 22 — The Animal That Broke the Rules

**Matrix:** story 18 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Billy · **Category:** Animals · **Arc:** How-it-works
**Stretch words:** electric, signal, category
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1060
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** Billy liked things to go in boxes.

Not actual boxes. Sorting boxes, in his head. Birds have feathers and lay eggs. Mammals have fur and drink milk. Fish live in water. Everything fits somewhere, and once you know which box a thing goes in, you know a lot about it without being told.

Then he saw a platypus, and the boxes fell over.

It happened at seven in the morning, which is the other end of the day from when everybody had been looking, and it happened because Billy had got up to go to the toilet and gone the long way round past the water.

It was out in the middle, floating. Small — much smaller than he expected, about as long as his forearm — dark brown and flat and shaped a bit like a chopping board with a tail. It rolled forward and went under with no splash at all.

He counted. Thirty-one seconds. It came up in a completely different place.

Then it floated there, chewing, with its eyes shut.

Billy did not go and get anybody, which he thought about afterwards and decided was correct, because by the time he'd got back it would have gone.

Now. Here is why that animal breaks every rule Billy had.

It has fur, and it feeds its babies on milk — so it's a mammal. But it **lays eggs**, which mammals are not supposed to do. Two, usually, soft-shelled, about the size of a marble.

It has no nipples. The milk comes out through the skin, and the babies lap it out of the fur.

It has a bill like a duck's, except it isn't hard like a duck's — it's soft, and rubbery, and warm.

It has webbed feet with claws underneath, and the webbing folds back when it walks so it can dig.

And the males have a spur on each back ankle with venom in it, which is very unusual for a mammal and extremely unusual for anything with fur that looks that comfortable.

Then there's the thing it does underwater, which is the best fact in the whole valley.

When a platypus dives, it shuts its eyes. It also shuts its ears and its nostrils. It is, for that entire thirty seconds, completely blind, deaf and unable to smell.

So how does it find anything?

Its bill can feel **electricity**.

Every time a living thing moves a muscle — a shrimp flicking, a worm bending — it makes a tiny **electrical** pulse in the water. Thousands of tiny sensors in that soft bill pick those pulses up. The platypus sweeps its bill side to side across the bottom like somebody sweeping a metal detector, and follows the **signals**, and grabs whatever made them.

It hunts by feeling the electricity of other animals thinking about moving.

Billy sat down on a rock and said, out loud, to nobody: "That's not allowed."

He told everyone at breakfast. Nobody believed the electricity part. Skye believed it immediately and then explained it back to everybody wrong, three times, getting closer each go.

Adi said "duck."

Which is exactly what the first scientists in England said, more or less, when a skin was sent over from here — that somebody had sewn a duck's bill onto a mole as a joke. It took years to convince people the animal was real.

"So what *is* it," said Rori.

And that's the interesting part, and Billy got there on his own by about lunchtime.

It isn't a broken mammal. It's an old one. The platypus and the echidna are the last two of a very old branch that split off before the rest of the mammals started giving birth to live young. They're not a mistake in the system. They're what the system looked like first, still going, in a river four hours from home.

The **categories** aren't wrong either. They're just something people invented afterwards, to help, and the animals were never asked.

**ECHO:** You saw her.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing in the leaves at the edge of the path. That's Echo. She turns up for people who've gone quiet, and Billy had been sitting on a rock not moving for about ten minutes.

**ECHO:** Nobody else did. You went the long way and you didn't shout.

**NARRATOR:** "I nearly shouted."

**ECHO:** I know. I was watching you not.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the leaves, found something, ate it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's a drawing one, so you need a stick and some dirt or a pencil and some paper.

Make up an animal out of parts. A bill from one thing, feet from another, a tail from something else. Give it something impossible — let it hear colours, or smell yesterday.

Draw it, and show somebody, and let them tell you it isn't allowed.

Then tell them about the platypus.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone, without a sound.

Adi's had eleven legs and was called Dog.
