# Story 28 — The Mud Dam

**Matrix:** story 31 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Billy · **Category:** Activities · **Arc:** Race
**Stretch words:** seep, foundation, current
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1070
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** It started as a competition, which most of the good ones do.

There was a place where a small trickle came down off the rocks and ran across a flat bit of clay before it joined the big water. Not a river. Barely a stream. About as wide as a plate and about as deep as a finger.

Rori dammed it with one handful of mud in roughly four seconds and shouted that she had won.

Billy pointed out that she had not won, because water was already coming through the middle of it.

Rori put more mud on. The water came round the side.

"You can't just *put mud on it*," said Billy.

"I *am* just putting mud on it."

"It's not *working.*"

"It's working a BIT."

And that is the entire history of engineering, performed by two five-year-olds in about ninety seconds.

So they raced. Two dams, twenty paces apart, one each, and the winner is whoever's dam is still holding when the grown-ups call everybody for lunch.

Here's what Rori did.

She built fast, out of whatever was nearest, in a straight line across the stream. It went up quickly and it looked, genuinely, impressive. It was the tallest thing either of them built all day.

Then the water arrived at it, and stopped, and went up, and started coming through the bottom.

That's **seep**. Water is patient in a way that is genuinely unsettling: it doesn't need a hole, it just needs a gap between two bits of dirt, and it will find every single one and slowly make them bigger. A dam doesn't usually fail at the top. It fails underneath.

Here's what Billy did.

Billy spent the first four minutes not building anything at all, which Rori found infuriating and mentioned several times.

He was digging. He scraped a shallow trench across the bed of the stream, down into the sticky grey clay underneath the loose stuff, and he packed that trench full of wet clay first, pressing it down with the heel of his hand until it went shiny.

That's a **foundation**. It's the bit nobody can see and the only bit that matters — you're not building a wall on the ground, you're joining the wall *to* the ground, so there's no seam for the water to find.

Then he built up from it, in layers, squeezing each layer onto the last, leaning the whole thing slightly upstream so the water pushed it into itself instead of over.

It took twenty minutes and it was half the height of Rori's and it looked like nothing much at all.

Rori's went at about the eleven-minute mark. Not dramatically — it just got quietly hollow underneath, and then a piece of the middle sat down, and then all of it went at once in a satisfying brown rush.

She said a word. Then she went and looked at Billy's.

And this is the bit worth telling.

She didn't sulk and she didn't start another one of her own. She said, "Show me the trench thing," and then she dug, and they built the second half of Billy's dam together, and it got much better once there were two of them, because one could hold while the other packed.

It held a pond about as big as a bathtub. There was a **current** running into it at one end and a slow lip of water going over a spillway they cut on purpose at the other, because Billy worked out — correctly, and on his own — that if you don't give the water somewhere to go, it will choose somewhere.

They put leaves on it and raced them.

Skye moved in as head of communications and told several unrelated families about the dam.

Adi destroyed a corner of it by sitting in it and was, after some discussion, forgiven.

**ECHO:** Somebody built one here before.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing where the clay went dry, watching the pond. That's Echo.

**ECHO:** Further down. Much bigger. Grey, and it's still there, and it made all of this.

**NARRATOR:** "The wall?" said Billy. "The big one?"

**ECHO:** Same idea as yours. Bigger trench.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the mud, found something, ate it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it's a wet one, so ask first.

Find a trickle. Any trickle — a stream, a gutter after rain, water running off a rock.

Build a dam that holds. Not a tall one. A *holding* one.

Dig the trench first. Pack the bottom before you build the top. Give the water a spillway at one side, on purpose, before it makes its own.

Then sit and watch it, and find the leak. There's always a leak. Finding it is the mission — fixing it is just the afternoon.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone.

The dam was still there the next morning, mostly. The leaf race is unresolved and both of them claim it.
