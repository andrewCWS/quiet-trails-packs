# Story 31 — Damper, Ash and Patience

**Matrix:** story 43 in the 50-story plan

**Mode:** Prose · **Key kid:** Skye · **Category:** Activities · **Arc:** How-it-works
**Stretch words:** knead, coals, embers
**Feeling:** not named in this one
**Word count:** ~1070
**Voices:** NARRATOR and ECHO. Echo sets the mission.

---

**NARRATOR:** Damper is bread made by people who have not got much.

Flour, water, a pinch of salt, and — if you're being fancy about it — a bit of butter and some milk. That's it. No yeast, no oven, no waiting overnight. You mix it, you shape it, and you cook it in the fire.

It's the sort of food that exists because somebody a long way from a shop was hungry and had a bag of flour, and it has stayed on the menu ever since because it is genuinely good and because there is something deeply satisfying about eating bread you cooked in a hole.

Skye was in charge of it, which she had arranged by asking eleven times.

The mixing went well. Flour in the bowl, water in a bit at a time, and then hands in — which is the whole point, and which cannot be done neatly and should not be attempted by anybody who minds.

Then the **kneading**, which is where the trouble started.

You push the dough down and away with the heel of your hand, fold it back over, turn it, and do it again. And again. For a few minutes.

It changes while you do it. At the start the dough is shaggy and sticky and comes apart. After a couple of minutes it goes smooth, and springy, and stops sticking to everything, and when you poke it, it pushes back.

Skye kneaded it four times and declared it done.

"It's not done."

"It's *bread*."

"It's not bread yet, it's still —"

"It LOOKS like bread."

It did look like bread. It looked exactly like bread. It also fell into three pieces when she picked it up, which settled the argument without anybody having to win it.

So she kneaded it properly, which took about four minutes, and complained for three and a half of them, and then stopped complaining because she'd felt it change under her hands and wanted to keep going to see how far it went.

Then the fire, and this is the part everybody gets wrong.

You do not cook damper on flames.

Flames are hot at the top and inconsistent and full of soot, and a loaf put over them goes black on the outside in four minutes and stays raw dough in the middle. Which is exactly what happened to the first one, and which everybody agreed to call "the practice one."

You cook it on **coals**. You let the fire burn down — properly down, until the flames have gone and what's left is a bed of orange-grey **embers** that are radiating a steady, even, ferocious heat with nothing leaping about.

Then you scrape a hollow, sit the dough in a camp oven or wrap it in foil or just lay it straight on the ash if you're brave, cover it, and leave it alone.

And "leave it alone" is the actual recipe. Twenty to thirty minutes without lifting the lid, because every time you look, the heat goes out and the clock starts again.

Skye lifted the lid twice in the first eight minutes.

Then Billy sat next to her and said, "How long's it been," in a way that was extremely annoying, and she stopped.

The way you know it's done is you tip it out and knock on the bottom of it with a knuckle. If it sounds hollow, it's cooked. If it sounds like a thud, it goes back.

Skye's went back once. The second knock was hollow and she made everybody come and listen to it.

It came out with a hard dark crust with ash on it, and inside it was white and steaming and dense in a way that shop bread never is, and they ate the whole thing standing up with too much butter, in about six minutes, having spent an hour and a half on it.

Which is the correct ratio and always has been.

**ECHO:** You knocked on it.

**NARRATOR:** A brown bird with a long untidy tail was standing at the edge of the firelight where the warm ground starts. That's Echo.

**ECHO:** Most people cut it open to check. Then it's open, and it's not finished, and it never comes back.

**NARRATOR:** "Billy told me to knock," said Skye. "Billy read it on the bag."

**ECHO:** Billy reads bags.

**NARRATOR:** She scratched at the warm ash at the edge, found nothing, and looked disappointed about it.

**ECHO:** Here's a mission, and it takes an hour and a half and gives you six minutes, which is the deal.

Make damper. Flour, water, salt, hands.

**Knead** it until it stops sticking to you and starts pushing back — and that takes longer than you'll want it to.

Wait for the flames to go. Cook it on the **embers**, not the fire.

And then don't look at it. Not once. That's the actual hard part, and the person who can do it should be in charge of it next time.

**NARRATOR:** And then she was gone.

The practice one got fed to nobody, because there was nothing living in that camp that wanted it.
